      <h2>Don't Skip Due Diligence.</h2>  
      <p>  
        It sits at the core of every smart real estate purchase. Yet in today's emotional market, it's the first thing buyers eliminate.  
      </p>  
        
      <hr style="border: 0; border-top: 1px solid #e2e8f0; margin: 32px 0;">  
  
      <h3 style="margin-top: 0; font-size: 20px;">What is Due Diligence?</h3>  
      <p>  
        Formally defined, <strong>due diligence</strong> is the investigation and exercise of care that a reasonable person is expected to take before entering into an agreement or contract.  
      </p>  
      <div style="background: #f1f5f9; padding: 20px; border-left: 4px solid #1e3a8a; margin: 24px 0; border-radius: 0 12px 12px 0;">  
        <h3 style="margin-top: 0; font-size: 18px;">How it is used in Real Estate:</h3>  
        <p style="font-size: 16px; margin-bottom: 0; color: #334155;">  
          In our industry, the "due diligence period" is a specific timeframe provided in the sales contract. It is your protected opportunity to fully examine the property, verify the seller's claims, inspect the physical condition, and confirm the financial numbers <em>before</em> your money is fully at risk.  
        </p>  
      </div>  
      <p style="margin-bottom: 0;">  
        Unfortunately, in today's emotion-driven market, many buyers are not fully educated on what due diligence is and how to properly navigate through it. As a buyer, you need to fact-find because real estate is inherently an <strong>inefficient marketplace</strong>. Information about a specific property is not readily available and is not always easy to find. Therefore buyers need adequate time to investigate before moving forward with a real estate purchase.  
      </p>  
    </section>
